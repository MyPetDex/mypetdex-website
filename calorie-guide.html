How It Works

The RER & MER Formula

MyPetDex doesn't guess at portions. Every recommendation starts from Resting Energy Requirement (RER) — the calories your pet's body burns at complete rest — then adjusts for how active they are and what life stage they're in.

RER = 70 × (body weight in kg)0.75 The 0.75 exponent accounts for metabolic scaling — smaller pets burn more calories per pound than larger ones.

From there, we calculate Maintenance Energy Requirement (MER) — the number that actually tells you how much to feed:

MER = RER × Activity Factor × Life Stage Factor Body condition score (BCS) is then layered on top to fine-tune for pets who need to gain, lose, or maintain weight.

Activity Multipliers

How Active Is Your Pet, Really?

This is the single biggest factor pet owners get wrong. A "moderate" estimate on the food bag rarely fits an individual pet.

Sedentary ×1.2

Rests most of the day, brief bathroom-break walks only. Typical for senior pets or those with mobility limits.

Moderate ×1.4

30–60 minutes of walking or play daily. Fits most household pets with a regular routine.

Active ×1.6

1–2 hours of daily exercise — hiking, running, swimming, or extended play sessions.

Very Active ×2.0

Working, sporting, or competition animals with 3+ hours of intense daily activity.

Life Stage Nutrition

Calorie Needs Change With Age

Puppies & Kittens ×1.75

Growing bodies need significantly more energy. Feed a growth-formulated food until fully grown (typically 12–24 months, depending on breed size).

Adults ×1.0

Maintenance needs. The focus shifts to holding a healthy weight and body condition long-term.

Seniors ×0.8

Metabolism slows with age, but protein needs stay high. Senior-formulated food with joint support is worth considering.

Special cases

Pregnancy, nursing, illness, and post-surgery recovery all change energy needs — these require a vet's guidance, not a calculator.

Adjusting Over Time

The Calculator Is a Starting Point

Every pet's metabolism is a little different. Recheck body condition every 2–4 weeks and adjust from there.

Ideal condition: Ribs easily felt but not visible, a visible waist from above, and an abdominal tuck from the side.
Gaining weight? Reduce daily portions by 10–15%.
Losing weight? Increase daily portions by 10–15%.
Sudden or dramatic changes? Talk to your vet — it could signal a health issue, not just a feeding one.
⚠️ Important: This calculator provides an estimate only and does not replace professional veterinary advice. Always confirm feeding plans with your vet, especially for pets with health conditions.

FAQ

Common Questions

How accurate is this calculator?

It uses the same RER/MER formula taught in veterinary nutrition programs and referenced by WSAVA. It's a strong starting point, but every pet's metabolism varies — treat the result as a baseline to adjust from, not a fixed rule.

Should I adjust for my pet's breed?

Some breeds run leaner or heavier metabolisms than average (sighthounds vs. brachycephalic breeds, for example). Start with the calculated number, then adjust based on body condition over a few weeks.

How often should I feed my pet?

Most adult dogs and cats do well on 2 meals a day. Puppies and kittens typically need 3–4 smaller meals to support growth and blood sugar stability.

Do treats count toward the daily total?

Yes — treats should make up no more than about 10% of daily calories. Large or frequent treats can throw off the whole calculation.
